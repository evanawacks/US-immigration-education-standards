u4 Overview
u5 In grade 5, students will explore how people moved, traded, and interacted with others in early civilizations. They will learn how geography, power, wealth, and religion shaped the growth and culture of these societies. Students will focus on regions like Western Asia, the Middle East, North Africa, and Sub-Saharan Africa. They will also learn about important cultural contributions and lasting achievements. By studying ancient history, students will better understand the roots of the modern world.
u6 Looking Back
u7 In grade 4, students examined change and continuity within Vermont and United States history from pre-colonial times through the Civil War. Students learned the concept of movement and its impact on people, goods, and ideas and how natural resources have influenced economic development. Students examined the conflicts and compromises that resulted from migration and settlement to understand the tensions and factors that led to the fight for independence, the establishment of the United States, and civil unrest.
u8 Looking Ahead
u9 In grade 6, students will again explore the world and ancient civilizations by examining how movement and migration impacted the interactions between expanding civilizations through conquest and trade, including the physical and political geography and ancient societies of South and East Asia, Oceania, Latin America, and Europe. Students will note how geography, power, wealth, and religion influenced culture, change, and development within civilizations as they examine how the perspectives of political science, economics, geography, history, and archaeology apply to the study of regions and countries.

u11 Inquiry
u12 Why inquiry? Using inquiry as an instructional model for the teaching of social studies allows students to authentically ask questions, search for and analyze evidence, and provide explanations and arguments to challenges facing their world.
u13 The inquiry instructional model is driven by authentic questions or problems that have relevance for children. Students learn content, skills, and dispositions as they seek answers to questions and as they apply their learning to take informed action. Pioneered by Dewey (1938), inquiry is an attempt to make classroom experiences feel more like the authentic, curiosity-driven experiences that motivate learning outside of school. (Using Inquiry to Prepare Students for College, Career, and Civic Life, elementary edition, p. 22)
u14 The Inquiry Arc is defined in Dimension 1 (questions), Dimension 3 (evidence), and Dimension 4 (communication) of the College, Career, and Civic Life (C3) Framework for Social Studies State Standards. In the AOE-developed Proficiency- Based Learning Social Studies Hierarchy, Dimensions 1, 2, and 3, as well as evidence indicators found within Dimension 2 – History, have been combined into a singular Critical Proficiency – the Inquiry Process. Opportunities for showing proficiency in the Inquiry Process fall within the K-2, 3-5, 6-8 grade bands of the Priority Performance Indicators (below): Constructing Compelling and Supporting Questions, Gathering and Evaluating Sources, and Communicating and Critiquing Solutions. The Inquiry Process Critical Proficiency, as with all of the CPs, begins its focus in the elementary grades and spans through high school, preparing students to attain proficiency in social studies for college and career readiness.
u15 The Critical Proficiency (CP) remains the same K-12 and identifies the enduring skills and knowledge that all students need to demonstrate (across contexts) to be prepared for future learning, including post-secondary career and college readiness. Priority Performance Indicators clarify what will be taught and assessed at each grade cluster.
