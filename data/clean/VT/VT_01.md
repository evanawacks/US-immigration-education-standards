Inquiry Process: Students act as social scientists as they ask questions, think critically, solve problems, and share conclusions.

Sample C3 Indicator Connections
Students will identify local community and government leaders that have roles and responsibilities to provide services for their community members. Students will be able to explain how communities have rules and people in charge to keep things fair and help everyone get what they need.
Civic Virtues and Democratic Principles: Applying Civic Virtues and Democratic Principles
D2.Civ.7.K-2. Apply civic virtues when participating in school settings.
D2.Civ.8.K-2. Describe democratic principles such as equality, fairness, and respect for legitimate authority and rules.
D2.Civ.9.K-2. Follow agreed-upon rules for discussions while responding attentively to others when addressing ideas and making decisions as a group.
D2.Civ.10.K-2. Compare their own point of view with others’ perspectives.
Students will understand that children, families, and communities exhibit cultural similarities and differences. Students will be able to identify examples of freedom, respect, and safety in their community and explain how these contribute to the common good. Students will understand that culturally, ethnically, and racially diverse people help to shape a community.
Rules, Processes, and Laws
D2.Civ.11.K-2. Explain how people can work together to make decisions in the classroom.
D2.Civ.12.K-2. Identify and explain how rules function in public (classroom and school) settings.
Students will identify examples of how individual actions (such as volunteering or voting) and government decisions (like making laws or providing services) help create order and promote peace in their community. Students will be able to identify characteristics of effective rules and participate in a process to establish classroom rules. Students can participate in problem solving, decision making and conflict resolution within their home, school, and community. Students will understand that outcomes to conflict or disagreements based on different beliefs, culture, and values can be achieved through compromise and cooperation. Students will describe how individuals can bring about change.

Students will understand that there were significant historical individuals and events that had an impact on Vermont. Students will be able to describe similarities and differences between families long ago and families today. Students will be able to explain ways that students can take informed action to help address issues and problems at school or in the community (See Civics - Rules, Processes, and Laws).

## Examples / clarifications

Overview
In grade 1, students will learn how they are connected to the world around them and develop a deeper understanding of how diverse cultures, values, and beliefs help to shape the world in which we live. Students will further develop a sense of purpose and empathy regarding their role and the role of other citizens within the community and world. As a global citizen, it is developmentally appropriate for students to identify similarities and differences from others (cultural, likes and dislikes, gender, skin color, hair, and eye color). Best practice is having materials and visuals that are representative of people, cultures, and the community in which they live. They will have the opportunity to look for ways that they can be civic-minded and make change happen within their own classroom, school, and community. Students will develop an understanding of how basic economic concepts influence people, places, and the environment. They will learn that people not only use the environment to meet their needs and wants but also that the environment often dictates where and how people choose to live. Students will continue building group skills, including roles, rules, voicing an opinion, and conflict resolution skills with adult support. They will enhance their knowledge of spatial awareness of their surroundings by furthering the use of geographic representations, tools, and terminology to process information about the world around them. Students will continue to compare life in the past to life today as they study the contributions of historical figures and how those figures have helped shape and change various communities and environments across the globe.

• There are differences between a rule and a law. • Various people make laws/rules. • There are consequences for not following laws/rules. • Laws/rules are not the same everywhere. • Various people enforce laws/rules. • There are differences between rights (freedoms of speech and religions) and responsibilities (paying taxes, obeying the law). • People can bring about change in the classroom and/or community.
