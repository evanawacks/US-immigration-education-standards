u1 Indian Education for All Connections - Montana Social Studies Standards
u2 The new Social Studies Standards provide educators with an excellent opportunity to integrate Indian Education for All in K-12 Social Studies instruction. This document contains excerpts of Social Studies Standards that offer relevant connections to contemporary and historic American Indian issues. To access all of the Social Studies Content Standards and Model Curriculum Guide Documents visit the K -12 Standards & Revision website.
u3 MONTANA CONTENT STANDARDS FOR SOCIAL STUDIES
u4 The content areas covered by the social studies standards include:
u5 SS.CG civics and government
u6 SS.E economics
u7 SS.G geography
u8 SS.H history

u23 SOCIAL STUDIES CONTENT STANDARDS FOR KINDERGARTEN
u24 (x=72) SS.G.K
u25 (x=135) The geography content standard for kindergarten is that each student will use maps, and other representations, to describe place characteristics.
u26 (x=72) SS.H.K
u27 (x=135) The history content standard for kindergarten is that each student will distinguish between past, present, and future time.
u28 SOCIAL STUDIES CONTENT STANDARDS FOR FIRST GRADE
u30 (x=135) The geography content standards for first grade are that each student will:
u32 (x=135) identify and describe human and physical local landmarks.
u34 (x=135) construct maps and other representations of familiar places.

u41 SOCIAL STUDIES CONTENT STANDARDS FOR SECOND GRADE
u42 (x=72) SS.CG.2
u43 (x=135) The civics and government content standards for second grade are that each student will:
u45 (x=135) explain the roles of people who help govern different communities including tribal communities.
u47 (x=135) The geography content standards for second grade are that each student will:
u49 (x=135) use the basic components of a map to identify physical and political features, including American Indian reservations.
u51 (x=135) describe how geography and human activities impact each other.
u53 (x=135) The history content standards for second grade are that each student will:
u55 (x=135) identify how people lived differently in the past than they do today.
u57 (x=135) understand that there are twelve distinct and unique tribes within Montana whose people contribute to modern life.
u59 (x=135) identify different kinds of historical sources, including oral histories of American Indians.
u60 SOCIAL STUDIES CONTENT STANDARDS FOR THIRD GRADE
u61 (x=72) SS.CG.3
u62 (x=135) The civics and government content standards for third grade are that each student will:
u64 (x=135) describe and identify the basic functions of local government, including tribal governments.
u66 (x=135) identify key symbols of nations.
u68 (x=135) The economics content standards for third grade are that each student will:
u70 (x=135) explain economic interdependence within historical and contemporary contexts.
u72 (x=135) The geography content standards for third grade are that each student will:
u74 (x=135) examine maps and other representations to identify historical and contemporary political and cultural patterns in the Americas.
u76 (x=135) identify environmental and technological events and conditions and how humans and the environment impact each other.
u78 (x=135) identify landforms and other physical characteristics of the Americas.

u87 SOCIAL STUDIES CONTENT STANDARDS FOR FOURTH GRADE
u88 (x=72) SS.CG.4
u89 (x=135) The civics and government content standards for fourth grade are that each student will:
u91 (x=135) describe how rules, laws, and policies are implemented by local, state, national, and tribal governments.
u93 (x=135) define sovereignty for tribes in Montana.
u95 (x=135) identify key foundational documents in Montana's government.
u97 (x=135) The economics content standards for fourth grade are that each student will:
u99 (x=135) explain how trade leads to increasing economic interdependence among groups in Montana.
u101 (x=135) The geography content standards for fourth grade are that each student will:
u103 (x=135) examine maps and other representations to explain the movement of people.
u105 (x=135) identify and label the tribes in Montana and their indigenous territories, and current locations.
u107 (x=135) investigate the physical, political, and cultural characteristics of places, regions, and people in Montana.
u109 (x=135) analyze environmental and technological events and conditions and how humans and the environment impact each other with relation to settlements and migration in Montana.
u111 (x=135) The history content standards for fourth grade are that each student will:
u113 (x=135) understand tribes in Montana have their own unique histories.
u115 (x=135) identify events and policies that have impacted and been influenced by tribes in Montana.
u117 (x=135) explain how Montana has changed over time given its cultural diversity and how this history impacts the present.
u119 (x=135) describe how historical accounts are impacted by individual perspectives.

u159 SOCIAL STUDIES CONTENT STANDARDS FOR SIXTH THROUGH EIGHTH GRADE
u160 (x=72) SS.CG.6-8
u161 (x=135) The civics and government content standards for sixth through eighth grade are that each student will:
u162 SS.CG.6-8.1 explain a variety of forms of government from the past or present.
u163 (x=72) SS.CG.6-8.2 explain the structure of and key principles in foundational documents, including
u164 (x=135) the Montana Constitution.
u165 (x=72) SS.CG.6-8.3 explain how global and American Indian civilizations and governments have
u166 (x=135) contributed to foundational documents of the United States.
u167 (x=72) SS.CG.6-8.4 distinguish the structure, organization, powers, and limits of government at the
u168 (x=135) local, state, national, and tribal levels.
u169 (x=72) SS.CG.6-8.5 identify events and leaders that ensure that key United States principles of
u170 (x=135) equality and civil rights are applied to various groups, including American Indians.

u221 THE SOCIAL STUDIES CONTENT STANDARDS FOR NINTH THROUGH TWELFTH GRADE
u222 (x=72) SS.CG.9-12
u223 (x=144) The civics and government content standards for ninth through twelfth grade are that each student will:
u224 (x=72) SS.CG.9-12.1 analyze and evaluate the ideas and principles contained in the foundational
u225 (x=144) documents of the United States, and explain how they establish a system of government that has powers, responsibilities, and limits.
u226 (x=72) SS.CG.9-12.2 analyze the impact of constitutions, laws, treaties and international agreements
u227 (x=144) on the maintenance of domestic and international relationships.
u228 SS.CG.9-12.3 evaluate the impact of international agreements on contemporary world issues.
u229 SS.CG.9-12.4 apply civic virtues and democratic principles when working with others.
u230 (x=72) SS.CG.9-12.5 evaluate how citizens and institutions address social and political problems at
u231 (x=144) the local, state, tribal, national, and/or international levels.
u232 (x=72) SS.CG.9-12.6 evaluate the American governmental system compared to international
u233 (x=144) governmental systems.
u234 (x=72) SS.CG.9-12.7 explain the foundations and complexity of sovereignty for federally recognized
u235 (x=144) tribes in Montana.
u236 (x=72) SS.CG.9-12.9 evaluate government procedures for making decisions at the local, state, national,
u237 (x=144) tribal, and international levels.
u238 (x=72) SS.CG.9-12.10 analyze historical, contemporary, and emerging means of changing societies,
u239 (x=144) promoting the common good, and protecting rights.
u240 (x=72) SS.CG.9-12.11 analyze the impact and roles of personal interests and perspectives, market,
u241 (x=144) media and group influences on the application of civic virtues, democratic principles, constitutional rights, and human rights.
u242 (x=72) SS.CG.9-12.12 evaluate citizens' and institutions' effectiveness in ensuring civil rights at the
u243 (x=144) local, state, tribal, national, and international levels.
