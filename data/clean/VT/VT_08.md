## Examples / clarifications

Overview
In grade 8, students will utilize past knowledge and understanding to expand their focus on the core concepts and principles underlying America’s democracy; the structure and limits of the national government as set forth in the United States Constitution, including an examination of democratic ideals; and the role of the citizen in a democratic society. Students will collaborate to identify a problem(s); develop solutions that would require government involvement; and participate in student-led civics projects focused on developing civic knowledge, skills, dispositions, and media literacy. Students will also learn about the workings of local, national, and global economies and develop an understanding of the decision-making processes of individuals, businesses, and governments and how they are connected. Gaining an understanding of economic resources, such as human capital, land, technology, and investments, and how they play a role in big-picture decision making in a global environment will assist students in making informed decisions in a wide variety of contexts.

Illicit: The Dark Trade - National Geographic: Resources are unevenly distributed across the surface of the Earth, and no one country has all the resources it needs. Therefore, each country must trade with others, resulting in a world of global economic interdependence—an exchange between “producers” (those who produce or manufacture goods) and “consumers” (those who purchase or use the goods). The two following activities help students understand the concept of economic interdependence between different countries, and understand terms such as trade, import, and export. A trading simulation will help students understand the conditions that encourage or discourage trade among countries. In
the activities, students will split into country groups and employ product-unit trading cards to represent the net worth of five world countries. They will use these trading cards to obtain goods not available in their country and to disperse goods that their country produces. Students will discover that some commodities are more desirable than others and that shifts in an economic market can directly affect the price of goods.
