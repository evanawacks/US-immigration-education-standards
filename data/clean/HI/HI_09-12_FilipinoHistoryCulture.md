## Examples / clarifications

Sample Compelling Questions:
●What makes you, you (ikaw/sika)? ●How do you contribute to your community (pamayanan/sangailian)?
●Early Filipino Cultural Identity: Indigeneity ●Cultural Geography: formal regions, archipelago ●Identity Formation: stages of ethnic identity, colonial mentality, decolonization, multiple identities, intersectionality
●Cultural Expression of Identity: music, dance, art, literature, performances ●Cultural Representation and Stereotype: comedy, propaganda, media ●Social Action: justice, equity, diversity, and identity (JEDI)
●Why does the past (kasaysayan/pakasaritaan dagiti pasamak) matter today? ●How do past interactions and connections influence the development of societies and cultures?
●Early Civilization: intercultural trade, maritime trade, Chinese trade interactions, Indian trade interactions, Islamic trade interactions; Southeast Asian trade interactions and connections ●Colonization of the Philippines: the arrival of Ferdinand Magellan and other colonizers, Hispanization of the Philippines, Christianity, Philippine Revolts (1521–1946), Thomasites and the Rise of English, education systems and policies, Commonwealth, WWII, Philippine Independence ●Rebellions, Revolutionary Leaders and Impacts (e.g., Lapu Lapu, Gabriela Silang, Illustrados (Filipino educated class), Jose Rizal, Apolinario Mabini, Andres Bonifacio, Emilio Aguinaldo, etc.)
●Western Influence in the Philippines: land rights, apellidos (surnames) and taxation, influence of religion, sociocultural changes, political changes, environmental changes, languages, food, brain drain ●Diaspora: overseas contract workers (OCW), foreign economic opportunities, balikbayan (Filipinos returning to Philippines)
●How does Filipino culture (kalinangan/kaanawidan) connect to other cultures? ●How do we build solidarity (pagkikipagkapwa/panagmaymaysa) with other groups?
●Practices: traditions (e.g., music, dance, food, celebrations), values (e.g. beliefs, perspectives, ideology), cultural (inter) connections (e.g., Native Hawaiian, Southeast Asian, East Asian, Spanish), language (e.g., Austronesian language connections)
●Past Influential Movements: People Power, Farm Workers movement, Ethnic Studies, Hawaii labor strikes ●New Movements: Pinayism or Peminism, Anti-Asian violence, movements for self-determination
● Why do people (tao/tattao) move? ● How do communities (pamayanan/sangilian) thrive (umuunlad/rumang-ay)?
●Causes of Migration: Imperialism (e.g., Louisiana Manilamen, Pensionados, citizenship, military service, Alaskeros (cannery), WWI & WWII military service, agricultural workers), policies (e.g., 1934 Tydings McDuffy Act, 1965 Immigration Act), economic causes (e.g., Hawaii Sugar Planters Association, cheap labor, occupational shortages) ●Movement within United States: ethnic enclaves (e.g., segregation, Little Manila)
●Past Significant Events: Plantation Era (e.g., Sakadas, unequal plantation hierarchy, Hanapēpē Strike and Massacre), World War 2 (e.g., First Filipino Infantry, 2nd Filipino Battalion) ●Past Filipino American Leaders and Impacts: Jose Libornio, Florentino Das, Farmworkers (e.g., Larry Itliong, Philip Vera Cruz, Pablo Manlapit), authors (e.g. Carlos Bulosan, Angeles Monrayo) ●Contemporary Filipino American Leaders and Impacts: education (e.g. Domingo Los Baños), politics (e.g., Simeon Acoba, Amefil Agbayani, Ben Cayetano), military (e.g., General Antonio Taguba), media (e.g., Emme Tomimbang), musicians ●Effects: Filipino identities (e.g., Fresh off the boat “FOB”, local identity, Filipinx, settler colonialism, settler Aloha ʻAina, Oceanic Filipinx) Community Leadership and Organizations (e.g., Filipino Community Center, Cultural Organizations, Filipino American Historical Society of Hawaii, Filipino American National Historical Society (FANHS)
●How would you define human rights (karapatan/kalintegan? ●What is your role and responsibility (tungkulin/akemen nga panagrebengan) as a Global Citizen?
●Human Rights Violations: racial discrimination, human trafficking, worker rights, gender equality and rights, religious freedoms, autocracies ●Proposed Solutions: affirm language and cultural rights (e.g., multiculturalism, language access, multilingualism, international and local policies)
●Causes of Climate Change: greenhouse gas and fossil fuels, typhoon, capitalistic economy, food system ●Consequences of Climate Change: extreme weather conditions, deforestation, land erosion, flooding, sea level rise, drought, coral bleaching, fishing ●Promoting a Culture of Sustainability and Resilience: traditional indigenous values, clean energy, local food, natural resource management, waste reduction, sustainable communities, green workforce, sustainable futures, protect biodiversity and endangered animals (e.g., Philippine eagle, water buffalo (carabao/tamaraw), culture and place-based education
● What makes a good community (pamayanan/sangailian)? ● How can young people (binhi/agtutubo) make a difference?
●Enduring Challenges: racism, socioeconomic inequality, education disparities, health care issues, social hierarchy ●Contemporary Responses: education reform (e.g., Operation Manong, Filipino Curriculum Project, Pamantasan, decolonial education, multicultural education, Social Emotional Learning), language (e.g., Seal of Biliteracy, multilingualism, heritage language learning), health care reform (e.g., mental health, culturally responsive health care education), diversity of employment opportunities, new social movements
●Civic Action: promoting social justice (e.g., community organizing, peaceful protesting), pedagogy (e.g., debate, problem-based education, student voice), participatory action (e.g., forming a club, providing a forum for debate, providing public testimony, running for office, volunteering, voting, writing letters to policymakers and the media)
