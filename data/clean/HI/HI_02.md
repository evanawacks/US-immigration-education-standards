## Examples / clarifications

Sample Compelling
• What makes a good leader? • How can I make a difference?
Questions
• School: principal, teacher, vice-principal • Community: cultural leaders, governor, mayor, religious leaders, state representatives • National: members of Congress, president • Responsibilities: decision-making, establish and uphold rules and laws, safety
• Historical Figures: George Washington, Abraham Lincoln, Susan B. Anthony, Eleanor Roosevelt, Martin Luther King Jr., Ruby Bridges • Democratic Values: common good, equality, freedom, justice, liberty, respect for individual rights
• People: Benjamin Franklin, Amelia Earhart, Gandhi, Jackie Robinson, Rachel Carson, Caesar Chavez, Nainoa Thompson, Malala Yousafzai • Events: Louisiana Purchase, Wright brothers first flight, Japanese American internment, nuclear testing in the Pacific, end of apartheid, invention of the smart phone, wireless technology
Sample Compelling
• Why do people interpret historical events differently? • Do humans help or hurt the environment?
Questions
• Positive Impacts: national parks, protection of endangered species, wetland conservation areas • Negative Impacts: deforestation, loss of water rights, pollution
• Colonizing New England: religious and economic reasons for migration, Native American communities, resistance to settlement, Squanto • Newsboys Strike: child labor laws, low wages, poor working conditions • History of Flight: kites, balloons, gliders, Wright Brothers, Boeing, NASA
• Perspectives: audience perspective, author’s point of view, personal experience • Historical Events: voyages of Columbus, first Thanksgiving, Kamehameha unites the Hawaiian Islands, Ruby Bridges and school desegregation
Sample Compelling
• Why are we dependent on others for some goods and services? • Are we using natural resources wisely?
Questions
• Locally: entertainment, music, pineapple, taro chips, tourism • Globally: cars, clothing, food, phones, toys
• Goods: clothing, food, homes, technology • Services: construction, education, entertainment, firefighting, medical care, police
• Goods: bridges, national parks, roads • Services: firefighting, national defense, parks and playgrounds, police, public education, sanitation
• Maps: latitude, longitude, physical map • Landforms: canyons, gulches, peninsula, volcanoes • Water: creeks, lakes, oceans, ponds, rivers, seas, streams • Vegetation: desert, forests, plains, swamps, tundra
• Natural Resources: energy sources (e.g., solar, coal and gas, wind), minerals, ocean, vegetation, water • Uses: energy, food, profit
Sample Compelling
• Why does the cost of goods and services sometimes change? • How do we solve problems?
Questions
• Scarcity of Resources: human, natural resources (e.g., petroleum products, fish, produce, land, water) • Cost: dependence on imported food and goods, depleted fisheries, high cost of food, higher cost of housing, higher gas and electricity costs, higher wages, need to hire non-local labor
• Local Resources: clean water, farms, homes, ocean, ranches • Global Resources: cars, clothing, electronics, gas, toilet paper • Daily Needs: water, food, shelter, clothing
• Solutions: beach cleanup, building shelters, food drives • Problems: homelessness, hunger, land conservation
• Costs: delayed gratification, sacrifice • Benefits: allows you to make large purchases, to be prepared for unforeseen circumstances
